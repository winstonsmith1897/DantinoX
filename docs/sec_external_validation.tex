\subsection{External Validation}
\label{sec:external_validation}

To verify that DantinoX's paradigm implementations are not artefacts of
a single codebase, we reproduce its discrete-diffusion and
autoregressive training with independent PyTorch
implementations, dllm~\cite{zhou2026dllm} (LLaDA) and
xLM~\cite{xlm2026eacl}, so that the framework is the only variable.
Both comparisons match, on both sides: architecture (512-d, 8~attention
heads, 12~layers, plain GELU FFN, T5 SentencePiece vocabulary of
32{,}128); loss formula (the $(1/t)$-weighted masked cross-entropy for
diffusion, standard next-token cross-entropy for AR); optimizer (AdamW,
identical learning rate, warmup, and cosine schedule); and data
(WikiText-103-raw-v1, 50M-token cap, 90/10 train/validation split,
262M-token budget).
As Figure~\ref{fig:cross_framework_validation} shows, the trajectories
track each other closely, with final losses within $1\%$ for diffusion
and $8\%$ for AR (the residue consistent with defaults DantinoX
retains, such as RMSNorm and weight tying, vs.\ xLM's LayerNorm and
untied embeddings). Matching xLM's FFN required only flipping
DantinoX's \texttt{use\_swiglu} flag; restoring the default gated
SwiGLU recovers a further $0.14$~nats of validation loss, an ablation
that in a single-paradigm codebase would require re-implementation. The
exercise also illustrates the pitfalls the system is designed to remove:
both reference codebases silently skip weight initialization, inflating
the initial loss by ${\sim}50\times$ until the embeddings are manually
re-initialized, whereas DantinoX ships correct defaults out of the box.
